# Refined Paper Text: Evaluation Results

## Mode-1: Ask & Verify Framework

The proposed Transformer-based Hallucination Detection and Correction System was evaluated under Mode-1 (Ask & Verify), which performs structured claim-level verification without autonomous correction. In this mode, the system first standardizes and refines the user’s medical query before generating a response using a transformer-based language model. The generated answer is then decomposed into atomic, self-contained factual claims using a Flan-T5-based extraction module. Each claim undergoes independent verification through multi-source semantic retrieval, cross-referencing biomedical evidence from the PubMed API and the MedHallu dataset. This claim-level validation framework ensures that every factual statement is evaluated against established scientific literature rather than assessing the response as a whole.

The effectiveness of Mode-1 was demonstrated across representative high-risk medical queries, including whether turmeric can cure cancer without chemotherapy, whether intermittent fasting can permanently cure diabetes, and whether COVID-19 vaccines cause infertility. In each case, the system successfully extracted and verified core medical claims against authoritative evidence. For example, it confirmed that turmeric (curcumin) has been studied for anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties but lacks clinical evidence as a standalone cancer cure, and it verified that there is no scientific evidence linking COVID-19 vaccination to infertility. Similarly, it validated that intermittent fasting alone cannot permanently cure diabetes and that comprehensive medical management is required. Performance metrics indicate strong semantic retrieval and structured extraction capability (SBERT Recall: 98%, MRR: 97%; ROUGE-L: 95.22), demonstrating Mode-1’s reliability as a hallucination detection and verification mechanism for AI-assisted healthcare communication.

## Mode-2: Analyze & Correct

Table 2 presents the results of Mode-2 evaluation, where AI-generated medical text is analyzed and verified at the claim level. In this mode, the system performs an initial semantic refinement to resolve pronouns and structural ambiguities, followed by the extraction of individual claims. Verification is conducted through a **Council of Experts**—a multi-persona pipeline involving seven specialized LLMs (e.g., Pharmacologist, Treatment Expert, Epidemiologist) that retrieve and analyze up to 14 distinct pieces of evidence. This ensemble approach allows the system to identify subtle hallucinations and replace them with factually grounded corrections while preserving the valid parts of the original response.

Across the evaluated examples, the framework successfully detected multiple hallucinated claims related to treatment effectiveness and institutional approvals. For instance, unsupported statements such as "Aspirin permanently cures diabetes" and false institutional mandates (e.g., "The European Space Agency mandates high-dose Vitamin D") were identified and corrected using verified biomedical knowledge. Similarly, exaggerated claims regarding "nanorobotic delivery systems" in ginger-based products were replaced with realistic clinical contexts. The final outputs demonstrate the system’s ability to refine AI-generated medical answers into safe, evidence-based reconstructions that align with reliable clinical knowledge and improve factual transparency.
